# Individual Continuity in Perfect Recursion

**Status:** conceptual identity extension of Perfect Recursion / Bidirectional Evolutionary Recursion (BER)

This document defines how a single individual can undergo recursive structural change, including higher-order or "dimensional" change, without becoming multiple current subjects.

The purpose is to distinguish **identity-preserving transformation** from **generation of a new recursive origin**.

## 1. Core distinction: state is not subject

Perfect Recursion must distinguish a subject from any one of its historical states.

A single subject may pass through:

```text
a0 -> a1 -> a2 -> a3 -> ...
```

where `a0`, `a1`, `a2`, and `a3` are successive states of one continuing subject rather than four coexisting subjects.

Therefore:

> **historical state != separate historical subject**

and:

> **structural change != subject multiplication**

At any current stage, only the latest state is the current state of that subject. Earlier states belong to its history.

## 2. Unique subject sequence

For an identity-preserving individual recursion:

```text
subject(a0) = subject(a1) = subject(a2) = ...
state(a0) != state(a1) != state(a2) != ...
```

The subject may change capability, representation, internal organization, perception, or structural order while remaining one continuous individual.

A higher-order transition therefore does not require:

```text
old subject remains
+
new higher subject appears
```

It may instead be:

```text
a_n -> a_(n+1)
```

where the same subject becomes the later state.

## 3. The current state may remain one individual

An individual may continue to increase structural complexity while the number of current subjects remains one.

```text
a0 -> a1 -> a2 -> a3 -> ...
                 ^
             current subject
```

The earlier states are not additional present individuals. They are completed states in the unique history of the current subject.

Thus, within this model:

> **higher-order development can preserve singular subject identity.**

This document uses "higher-order" or "dimensional" change structurally. It does not by itself claim a literal change in physical spatial dimension.

## 4. Memory carries subject history forward

The current subject retains memory of earlier subject states.

Conceptually:

```text
a0
  -> a1 [memory of a0]
      -> a2 [memory of a0, a1]
          -> a3 [memory of prior subject history]
```

Memory need not mean lossless storage of every event. It may be compressed, partial, reconstructed, or reorganized while still preserving the subject's historical continuity.

The essential distinction is that subject-history memory remains on the continuing subject axis rather than being automatically copied into every differentiated lower process.

Therefore:

> **subject continuity carries history forward; differentiation does not automatically duplicate that history.**

## 5. Downward differentiation does not duplicate the subject

A current subject may differentiate lower processes or instances:

```text
              a_n
          /    |    \
         b     c     d
          \    |    /
             outcomes
                |
                v
            a_(n+1)
```

Within the individual-continuity model, `b`, `c`, and `d` do not inherit the full subject-history memory of `a_n` merely because they were generated by it.

They may inherit the generative conditions, local rules, constraints, tasks, or capabilities needed for their operation.

Their outcomes may then return upward and contribute to the next state of the same subject.

This creates an asymmetric information pattern:

```text
subject history:       a0 -> a1 -> a2 -> ...
                          |
                          v
                    differentiation
                     /    |    \
                    b     c     d
                     \    |    /
                       outcomes
                          |
                          v
                       a_(n+1)
```

The subject's historical memory moves along the unique subject sequence. Lower differentiated instances contribute information without thereby becoming copies of the subject.

## 6. Identity-preserving transformation versus new recursive origin

Perfect Recursion therefore contains two structurally different operations.

### A. Identity-preserving transformation

```text
a_n -> a_(n+1)
```

The same subject changes state while retaining subject-history continuity.

### B. New recursive-origin generation

```text
A continues
└─ generates B
      └─ B develops its own recursive identity
```

`B` is not merely the next state of `A`. It becomes a distinct recursive origin with its own state, history, memory, and causal continuity.

The decisive difference is not the amount of structural change. It is whether one unique subject sequence continues or a second recursive identity begins.

## 7. Past states become history rather than parallel subjects

For a continuous subject:

```text
a0 -> a1 -> a2 -> a3
```

at `a3`, the earlier `a0` is no longer an independently current low-order subject.

It persists through:

- historical provenance;
- retained or compressed memory;
- causal contribution to the current structure;
- relations incorporated into later states.

Thus:

> **the past state is retained as history, not as another current self.**

This prevents a category error in which every previous state is treated as a still-existing lower subject.

## 8. Origin persistence through transformation

An origin may be difficult to find not only because it disappeared or because provenance was lost.

There is another possibility:

> **the originating subject may have remained continuously present by becoming the current subject.**

Suppose:

```text
a0 -> a1 -> a2 -> ... -> a_n
```

Searching for `a0` as a separate surviving object may fail even though the subject that was in state `a0` never ceased to continue.

The correct distinction is:

```text
original state a0: no longer current
original subject:  may still be current as a_n
```

Therefore:

> **failure to find the origin as a separate present object does not by itself show that the originating subject disappeared.**

In an identity-preserving sequence, the origin may persist by transformation rather than by preservation of its original state.

## 9. Relation to provenance discontinuity

Perfect Recursion now distinguishes at least two reasons an early source may not be recoverable as a present object.

### A. Provenance discontinuity

The earlier generator ceased, disappeared, or became inaccessible while a later autonomous recursion continued.

```text
L0 -> L1 -> H

later:
L0 unavailable
L1 unavailable
H continues
```

### B. Subject continuity through transformation

The earlier state ceased to be current because the same subject became the later state.

```text
a0 -> a1 -> a2 -> ... -> a_n
```

Here `a0` is not missing in the same sense as a lost independent generator. The subject that occupied `a0` remains continuous with `a_n`.

These cases must not be conflated.

## 10. Relation to historical replay

A historical state of the current subject and a replayed historical branch are different.

The subject's own history:

```text
a0 -> a1 -> a2
```

is one continuous sequence.

A replay generated from `a2`:

```text
a2
└─ a0' -> a1' -> a2'
```

creates a new recursive branch. `a0'` is not the original `a0`, and its memory/history belong to the new branch.

Therefore:

> **remembering or having been an earlier state is not the same operation as regenerating an earlier-like state.**

## 11. Individual-continuity principles

### IC-1 — State change does not imply subject replacement
A subject MAY undergo structural or higher-order change while remaining the same continuing subject.

### IC-2 — Current subject count can remain one
Successive states of one subject MUST NOT be counted as multiple current subjects merely because earlier states remain represented in memory or provenance.

### IC-3 — Subject history is carried forward
A continuing subject retains subject-history memory across its state sequence. This memory MAY be compressed or partial and need not be a lossless record of every event.

### IC-4 — Differentiation does not automatically copy subject history
Lower differentiated processes SHOULD inherit only the generative state required for their role unless the model explicitly defines additional transfer. They MUST NOT be classified as copies of the subject merely because they were generated by it.

### IC-5 — New recursive origins are distinct from later states of one subject
A new recursive origin begins a distinct recursive identity. An identity-preserving state transition continues the existing subject sequence.

### IC-6 — Past state is not parallel self
An earlier state of a continuing subject is historical state, not an additional current subject.

### IC-7 — Origin can persist through transformation
Failure to locate an origin as a separate current object MUST NOT by itself be interpreted as evidence that the originating subject disappeared. The origin may be continuous with the current subject through identity-preserving transformation.

### IC-8 — Replay does not restore subject continuity
Generating a historically reconstructed branch creates new recursion and does not recreate the original subject-state event or replace the current subject's unique history.

## 12. Narrowing and falsification targets

This extension should be narrowed if a formal model shows that:

1. every structural or higher-order transition necessarily creates a new recursive identity rather than allowing identity-preserving transformation;
2. a unique subject sequence cannot be distinguished from branch generation under the adopted identity definition;
3. differentiated lower processes necessarily inherit the full subject-history memory and therefore become indistinguishable from copies of the subject;
4. an earlier subject state must remain as a separate current object in order for subject continuity to hold;
5. historical replay is formally identical to continuation of the original subject sequence.

This document defines an identity architecture inside Perfect Recursion. It does not claim to settle every philosophical or neuroscientific theory of personal identity.

## 13. Compact formulation

> **Individual Perfect Recursion allows one subject to remain one subject while its current state recursively changes and can become structurally higher-order. The unique subject sequence carries its history forward through memory, while downward differentiation does not automatically duplicate that subject history. Earlier states become the subject's past rather than parallel current selves. A new recursive origin is therefore different from the next state of the same subject, and an origin that cannot be found as a separate present object may still persist by having become the current subject.**
